# Product

## Register

product

## Users

ClockBill serves independent professionals and freelancers in Israel, Europe, and the United States. They move quickly between clients, projects, tasks, and billing arrangements, often while doing the work itself. Their core job is to capture every billable detail with as little interruption as possible, then understand what has been worked, billed, collected, and left behind.

## Product Purpose

ClockBill turns work activity into a complete, trustworthy money trail. It helps freelancers record time and billable items quickly, retain the detail needed for clients and reports, and avoid revenue leakage caused by forgotten hours, missed charges, or incomplete records. Success means that logging work feels effortless and users can see where their money stands without reconstructing it later.

## Brand Personality

Inviting, fast, and precise. The product should feel calm enough for daily use, direct enough for people in a hurry, and rigorous enough to trust with income-related records.

## Anti-references

Avoid the coldness of a developer infrastructure dashboard, the density and anxiety of enterprise finance software, and generic SaaS marketing built from vague claims, decorative gradients, or interchangeable feature-card grids. Avoid visual novelty that slows down the primary act of recording work.

## Design Principles

1. Make completeness feel effortless: guide users toward recording every billable detail without adding friction.
2. Explain through the workflow: demonstrate how work becomes an accurate financial record instead of listing disconnected features.
3. Show the money trail: keep worked, billed, paid, and outstanding amounts legible and connected.
4. Earn trust with precision: use clear language, predictable actions, explicit states, and accurate formatting.
5. Welcome before asking: make the marketing experience approachable and useful before requesting registration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA across public and authenticated surfaces, including keyboard and screen-reader use, reduced motion, sufficient contrast, zoom and reflow, touch targets, and non-color status cues. Preserve first-class Hebrew RTL and English LTR behavior. Evaluate delivery against applicable Israeli accessibility requirements, the European Accessibility Act and EN 301 549 context, and United States ADA and Section 508 expectations. Legal applicability can vary by operator and jurisdiction, so implementation should meet the strongest practical shared baseline without making unsupported compliance claims.
